Chronicles

A digital logbook that adapts to the work.

I worked with a team on Chronicles, a web application for keeping a structured record of project work. It brings custom entry formats, time logging, tasks and progress views into one workspace.

Context
University of the Witwatersrand
Module
Software Design Project III · COMS3011A
My role
Frontend development

My contribution

Frontend development.

My focus was the frontend: the user-facing side of Chronicles, where people interact with projects, entries and progress views. I worked as part of the project team; the features described here reflect the application we built together.

The problem

A useful record, wherever you work.

The assignment started with a student who relied on a paper logbook but often left it at home. They needed a quicker way to record completed work, see what remained, and understand how their time was being spent.

A fixed form would not suit every project. The central challenge was letting each project define its own entries while keeping the record searchable and useful.

What we built

One workspace, different ways to work.

Chronicles separates completed-work entries from outstanding tasks. Users can define project fields, capture entries and time, filter the record, and review work through boards, a calendar and statistics.

The dashboard brings selected views together. The app also includes Mira, an assistant interface for helping users navigate project work.

01

Custom project formats

Define fields such as text, numbers, dates, choices, tags and checklists, with links and calculated values available in the schema builder.

02

A searchable work record

Capture completed work and time, filter by project or custom fields, save useful filters, and keep outstanding tasks distinct from the record.

03

Progress you can see

Review logged time, activity and streaks, group entries on a board, and arrange dashboard panels around the information that matters.

The engineering challenge

The key design problem was consistency across changing data formats: an entry form, search view and statistics panel all need to understand the fields defined by the user. The schema editor is the starting point, with the rest of the experience adapting around each project’s structure.

Fracture Run

A 3D escape through a failing glass tower.

For our Computer Graphics and Visualisation group project, we built a browser-based game inspired by Smash Hit and Temple Run 2. The brief called for three levels; our game follows a character escaping through a glass tower, combining forward movement, aimed projectiles and environmental hazards.

Module
Computer Graphics and Visualisation
Format
Three main sectors + rooftop finale
Focus
Lighting, 3D models & physics
  • Three.js
  • WebGL
  • JavaScript
  • GLSL shaders
  • HTML & CSS

The concept

Movement meets precision.

The player travels through Ascension Tower as its systems fail, throwing a limited supply of energy spheres to break glass and clear hazards. Lane changes, jumps, slides and slow-motion aiming turn movement and timing into part of the challenge.

The influences serve different purposes: Temple Run 2 informs the running and obstacle navigation, while Smash Hit informs projectile aiming, breakable glass and ammunition management.

What we explored

Graphics as part of the gameplay.

We brought together lighting, materials, scene and character models, collision behaviour and visual effects. Glass is both a rendered surface and an obstacle; fire, smoke, moving machinery and falling structures shape what the player sees and how they respond.

First-person and chase cameras give the sectors different perspectives, with elevator transitions connecting the main environments.

The three levels

A different challenge in each sector.

01

Basement · Chase camera

The Shifting Foundry

Moving machinery and a third-person chase view introduce lane changes, jumps and slides through an industrial environment.

02

Labs · Environmental hazards

The Meltdown

Burning laboratories, smoke and a power outage make visibility and survival central to the escape, with a failing launcher as the player’s tool.

03

Skybridge · First-person aiming

The Skyline

Limited spheres, breakable glass and a collapsing skybridge demand accurate aiming and resource management before the final climb.

Beyond the three sectors: a rooftop survival finale leads to the helicopter escape. A separate Endless mode revisits the environments with repeatable runs.

Graphics & simulation

The technical work behind the game.

01

Lighting & glass materials

Custom GLSL shaders explore Fresnel reflection, refraction, dispersion and absorption in glass. Sun shadows, reflections and wet floors help give the environments depth.

02

Models, cameras & scenes

Player figures, machinery and distinct environments give each sector its own identity. Chase and first-person views change how players read space and aim.

03

Physics & visual effects

Projectile arcs, ricochets, collisions and GPU-simulated glass shards connect player actions to the scene. Volumetric fire, smoke and particles add environmental feedback.

Balancing visual detail with real-time play.

The rendering pipeline combines screen-space refraction, bloom, anti-aliasing, heat haze and power-up effects. Quality presets and dynamic resolution let the rendering workload scale, while reduced-motion and camera-shake settings offer control over the presentation.

Group project · Three.js and WebGL rendering, JavaScript gameplay, custom GLSL shaders and an HTML/CSS interface. This overview follows the project README and describes the team’s application.
